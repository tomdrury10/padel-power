What equipment do you need for padel?

Guides·18 May 2026·Padel Power

Ready to Try Padel? Here’s the Equipment You Actually Need

Padel is one of the fastest-growing sports in the UK, and once you play, it’s easy to see why. It’s social, easy to learn, fast-paced, and genuinely fun for all ages and fitness levels.

Played in doubles on an enclosed court, padel combines elements of tennis and squash, but with a much gentler learning curve. Beginners can enjoy long rallies from their very first session, which makes it ideal whether you’re completely new to racquet sports or switching from tennis.

The best part? You don’t need loads of equipment to get started.

In this guide, we’ll walk through the essentials, from choosing your first racket to finding the right shoes and accessories, so you can step onto court with confidence.

Choosing Your First Padel Racket

Your racket is the most important piece of equipment, and choosing the right one can make learning the game much easier.

Padel rackets come in three main shapes:

  • Round, Best for beginners thanks to a larger sweet spot and better control
  • Teardrop, A balance of control and power
  • Diamond, Designed for advanced players looking for maximum power

If you’re new to the sport, a round racket is usually the safest choice.

Weight matters too. Most rackets range between 340g and 380g. Lighter rackets feel easier to manoeuvre and are more forgiving on the arm, while heavier models can generate more power but require better technique.

For most beginners, comfort and control should come before power.

You’ll also notice different materials:

  • Fibreglass rackets tend to feel softer and more forgiving
  • Carbon fibre rackets are stiffer, more durable, and often suited to experienced players

If possible, try a few different rackets before buying. What feels right is often more important than the specs on paper.

Do You Need Special Padel Balls?

Yes, but don’t overthink it.

Padel balls look similar to tennis balls, but they’re slightly less pressurised, which helps suit the smaller court and style of play.

Most clubs provide balls for social games and coaching sessions, so beginners usually don’t need to buy their own straight away.

Once you start playing regularly, it’s worth keeping a tube in your bag. Fresh balls improve bounce, consistency, and overall gameplay.

In colder outdoor conditions, balls can lose pressure more quickly, so regular players often replace them every few matches.

Choosing the Right Shoes for Padel

Good footwear makes a huge difference in padel.

The game involves quick direction changes, short sprints, and lots of side-to-side movement, so regular running trainers usually don’t provide enough support.

Here’s what to look for:

Non-Marking Soles

Most indoor courts require non-marking soles to protect the surface and provide reliable grip.

Lateral Support

Padel involves constant sideways movement, so stability around the ankle and midfoot is important.

Comfort and Breathability

Choose lightweight shoes with breathable materials to stay comfortable during longer sessions.

Proper Fit

Your shoes should feel secure without being tight. A poor fit can lead to blisters or instability during movement.

If you’re just starting out, many clubs offer shoe rental or allow clean indoor trainers until you decide to invest in dedicated padel shoes.

Essential Padel Accessories

You don’t need loads of extras when starting out, but a few small accessories can improve comfort and performance.

Overgrips

Overgrips improve comfort and help maintain control, especially during longer sessions or warmer weather.

Water Bottle and Towel

Padel is fast-paced and surprisingly physical. Staying hydrated matters more than most beginners expect.

Racket Bag

A basic racket bag helps protect your equipment and keeps everything together.

Breathable Sportswear

Comfortable gym clothing is absolutely fine when starting. As you play more regularly, you may prefer lightweight sportswear designed for court movement.

Eye Protection (Optional)

Final Thoughts: Just Get on Court

The truth is, you don’t need perfect equipment to start enjoying padel.

A beginner-friendly racket, comfortable shoes, and a good attitude are enough to get going. Most clubs offer rental equipment, so you can try the sport before investing in your own gear.

The biggest step is simply booking that first session.
